# Concepts

This document describes key concepts used throughout the project.

| Concept | Description | Related Concepts/Terms |
|---------|-------------|------------------------|
| Modular Assembly | The bee house is designed as a modular system where individual cylinder modules can be removed from the carrier body for cleaning or harvesting. | Cylinder Module, Carrier Body |
| Twist-and-Lock Mechanism | A method for securing the cylinder modules into the carrier body using a locking nub and a corresponding groove. | Locking Nub, Locking Groove |
| Integrated Mounting | The carrier body features a built-in slot that mates directly with a wall mounting bracket, eliminating the need for a separate backing plate. | Carrier Body, Wall Mounting Bracket |
| Multi-color Printing | The cap of the cylinder module is designed as an assembly of separate parts (stamen, petals, background) to allow for multi-color 3D printing to attract bees. | Cylinder Module |
| Candle Layout Strategy | The pluggable mechanism (`FaceLayout` in `src/manora/face_plate_layouts.py`) that decides where candle holes are centered on a face plate. It returns only candle center points; hole diameter and screw-hole presence are per-layout knobs. Layouts are selectable via `--layout` in `src/manora/generate_manora.py`. | Face Layout, Candle Spacing |
| Automatic Same-Level on the Top Face | Because the die rests on one face, the opposite (lit) face is horizontal, so every candle on it sits at the same height regardless of its 2-D position. The halachic "same level" rule constrains holder heights, not the 2-D footprint; conflating the two (halachic world-level vs. face-local y-coordinate) is a known error to avoid. | Same Level, Face Layout |
| Candle Spacing vs. Face Capacity | On the fixed 9-inch triangular face, the 8-candle face is carried by the single concave-down parabolic arch (the `downward_arc_layout`): all 8 regular candles placed from the bottom right up the right side, over the crown at (0, 95), and down the left side to the bottom left, with adjacent centers 26.62 mm apart (≥ 1.0 in) and the shamash ≈ 70.4 mm from the nearest regular candle. (Earlier capacity studies — straight row ≤ 7, horizontal arc ≤ 8–9, full circle ≤ 12, staggered/packed rows ≤ 15 at the 1.0-in minimum — were computed at the former smaller face size and are superseded by the 9-in face; the method and history are in `memory-bank/arrangement_approach.md`.) | Candle Spacing, Face Layout |
| Shamash Visibility Through the Candle Forest | When a fully populated face is viewed from its base edge toward the point at eye level with the top of the die, the candles form a "forest" that can occlude the shamash at the point. The innermost candle tops (the arc-top pair straddling the arch crown) must be separated by at least 0.5 in so a clear line of sight to the shamash remains. | Arc-Top Separation, Candle Layout Strategy |
| Numeric Arrangement Optimization | The method used to explore candle-arrangement feasibility: exact geometric modeling (corner-based holder fit, connector-clearance half-planes, symmetry), an epigraph (slack-variable) max-min formulation, multi-start SLSQP with strict verification, and sensitivity sweeps to find design levers. Optimized coordinates become hard-coded ground truth. Full detail in `memory-bank/arrangement_approach.md`. | Candle Layout Strategy, Candle Spacing, Shamash Visibility Through the Candle Forest |
| Ritual Ordering vs. Layout Shape | The left-to-right ritual (night-one candle on the right, add leftward, light left-to-right) is self-evident in a straight row, remains monotonic in an arc, and requires an explicit ordering convention (e.g., row-major front-to-back) for a circle or packed pattern. This is a ritual-clarity constraint, not a "level" constraint. | Left-to-Right Ordering, Candle Layout Strategy |
